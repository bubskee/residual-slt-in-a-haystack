# Style guide

These are the house conventions for this paper and the papers that follow it. When a rule here conflicts with a journal's template, the template wins for that submission. Otherwise, follow this guide.

## Typography

- **Class.** `amsart`, 11pt, letter paper.
- **Text and maths fonts.** Palatino text with Euler maths. The preamble has two branches that produce the same faces:
  - pdfLaTeX (arXiv, `latexmk`): `newpxtext` + `eulervm`, with full `microtype` (protrusion, expansion and small-caps tracking).
  - XeTeX (tectonic): `fontspec` with TeX Gyre Pagella loaded by file name, `eulervm` for maths, and `microtype` protrusion only. XeTeX does not support font expansion or tracking.
- **Measure.** `textwidth=31pc`, about 70 characters per line. Don't widen it. Fix any overfull lines locally.
- **Line spacing.** `\linespread{1.04}`, with `\emergencystretch=2.5em`.
- **Margin.** 1.15in wide, used only for the `\leantag` marks.
- **Headings.**
  - Sections use amsart's centred small caps.
  - Run-in `\paragraph` heads are small caps, with half a line of space above.
  - Theorem heads are **bold small caps** (`scplain` style), and the statement is italic.
- **Title page.** amsart's 42pt top skip is patched down to 6pt. The date, MSC 2020 codes and keywords go in the first-page footnote.
- **Links.** Coloured `linkink`, a dark blue (rgb 0.12, 0.22, 0.48), for internal links, citations and URLs.
- **Citations.** `natbib` author–year, in round brackets, with `plainnat`. Use `\citet` when the author is the subject of the sentence and `\citep` otherwise. Put locators in the optional argument: `\citep[Theorem 2.10]{lin2017}`.
- **Figures.** Generated by a script in `figures/`, set in Palatino, with Palatino-italic maths and monochrome only. Each script also checks the values it draws. Commit the PDF, the SVG and a 220-dpi PNG.
- **Tables.** Use `booktabs` rules only, with no vertical rules.
- **Inline maths that must stay on one line.** Wrap short formulas in `\mbox{$…$}` when a break inside them would split a fraction or a tuple, for example `\mbox{$(L+r)/(4r)$}`.
- **URLs.** Use `url` (not `xurl`) with `\urlstyle{same}`, so URLs break only at punctuation and are set in the text face; underscores are allowed break points. Put a long repository URL on its own centred line. Lean declaration names use `\leanname{…}` (typewriter, breakable at `_`).
- **Page breaks.** One `\clearpage` only, before the appendices. Appendix sections run on; never force a break that leaves a near-empty page.
- **`\R`.** Defined as `{\mathbb R}`, braced so that `\int_\R` works under `unicode-math`.

## Lean marks

- **What gets a mark.** Every theorem with a Lean certificate gets a `\leantag{File}{line}` immediately after its `\label`.
- **What the mark shows.** It prints LEAN ✓ in small caps and the certificate's file name underneath. The ✓ links to that line at the pinned snapshot.
- **Which commit links point to.** Tags link to the commit whose Lean source is pinned. Appendix E pins the reproducibility snapshot. Once the Lean changes, update both.
- **What gets no mark.** Remarks proved only on paper get no tag, and Section 7 says so explicitly.

## Epistemic labels

Each claim gets exactly one of these labels. Never blur them.

| Label | Meaning | How the paper signals it |
|---|---|---|
| Lean-certified | A Lean declaration, audited to the standard three axioms | A margin tag, plus a Section 7 entry |
| Proved here | A full argument in the text or an appendix | Theorem, Remark or Appendix |
| Cited | Rests on a reference | `\citep` with a locator |
| Interpretation | A standard reading that the proofs don't use | Say so explicitly: "we use this only as an interpretation" |
| Heuristic or conjecture | Not proved | Name it as heuristic or conjectural, and keep it out of theorem environments |

- **Scope of Lean certificates.** They are stated for ball localizations with bounded measurable densities. The transfer to smooth cutoffs is done on paper. Every summary of the Lean coverage must say this.
- **The population caveat.** Results about integrals of the population loss must never be described as statements about estimators. Learning-coefficient estimators such as Lau et al. work with the empirical loss and SGLD.

## Prose

The structural rules. Prose taste is not legislated here; see Defaults.

- **One scope statement.** The paper has exactly one scope paragraph: the last paragraph of the introduction (what the model is, what the theorems show, what they do not cover). Do not restate it in remarks, section endings or the formal-verification section; a remark may add at most one clause about its own limits.
- **Further directions.** A short closing section before the appendices may name natural extensions, open questions and the work in progress that follows the paper, with a citation where a draft exists. It states directions, not exclusions, so it is not a second scope paragraph.
- **Define once, define before use.** Each object gets one definition, and no term of art or coined term ("active", "inactive group") appears before it is defined. The introduction may give one plain gloss of (λ, m); Section 2 defines it.
- **Modelling choices that change the answer** (raw weights) get one clear sentence in Section 2 and are not repeated elsewhere. Minor conventions go in footnotes.
- **Voice and numbers.** "We" for the author. Present tense for results, past tense only for history. Exact fractions, such as 13/12, never decimals.
- **Word lint.** Do not use "novel", "crucially", "it is worth noting", "interestingly", "delve", "leverage".
- **AI disclosure.** Keep the disclosure paragraph in Appendix E, following the Leiden Declaration, and name the tools that were used.

## Defaults

Tendencies to lean on when unsure. They are not rules, and a passage that reads better without them wins.

- Open the abstract and the introduction with the concrete surprise rather than the setting.
- Say why something happens in words before the theorem that proves it.
- Short declarative sentences, one claim each. No rhetorical questions.
- Let a fact end a paragraph. Sentences that announce the paper's organization or importance ("Both questions then have exact answers", "Several directions follow") can usually be deleted once the preceding sentence has made the claim.
- Hedge where the hedge belongs and once. In proofs, state the step that handles a case rather than reassuring the reader that it is handled.

## Build

- `tectonic --keep-logs main.tex` is the reference build. `latexmk -pdf main.tex` must also work.
- After a change, rebuild `main.pdf` and refresh `main.bbl` (`tectonic --keep-intermediates`). The build must produce no undefined references and no overfull boxes wider than 3pt.
